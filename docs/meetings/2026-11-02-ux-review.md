## Summary

### 1. What Kevin demoed (current site)
The goal was to get a fan from **point A (landing page) to point Z (a booked trip)** in as few steps as possible, following Steven's earlier feedback.

- **"Plan my trip" with no sign-in.** A shorter Budget Calculator wizard: three steps give an estimate and show the partners who can deliver it. A guest still has to sign up before booking ("an extra step I'd prefer wasn't there"). A signed-in fan goes straight from *Save plan* to payment.
- **Hero linked to the stadium on screen.** "View matches" and "Plan my trip" use whichever stadium is showing in the carousel. Kevin said this "could be more clear".
- **Featured packages on the landing page.** Admin builds them; each shows price, what's included and slots booked/remaining (for urgency). *Book now* goes to checkout in one or two steps.
- **Community + tribes on the landing page.** Visitors can read posts and see tribes and member counts without signing in. Joining takes them into the fan dashboard.
- **Partner hubs.** "Get started" on a partner page goes straight into that partner's packages.
- **Smaller fan dashboard sidebar.** Now just Plan trip, Itineraries, Match schedule, Tickets and Financing.
- Kevin wants schools/other tournaments added to the hero's quick access. The hero needs more content (Ecobank, schools), which he is waiting on from the team.

### 2. Feedback on the current site
Everyone liked it: "very catchy, it draws you in" (Steven); "150, if not 200%" (Eric); "shaping up quite well" (Tony). The concerns were nearly all about **mobile**:

- **The stadium carousel is lost on mobile.** The green tournament widget (countdown, AFCON details, View matches / Plan my trip) stays full size and covers the background. Steven suggested moving that information somewhere else, or into small icons on the right, so the backdrop shows.
- **Make the logo bigger.** "It carries the aspirations."
- **The hamburger menu is hard to find.** First-time users won't think to tap the three bars. Steven wants an "idiot-proof" menu with plain labelled buttons, e.g. *I want to travel / My travels / Our partners / Itineraries / Tournaments & events / Ticketing*, for people who aren't sports fans or tech-savvy.
- **Always offer a way back.** Users two or three screens deep need a Home or Back button that is always there.
- **Choose what to drop on mobile.** Tony: responsive design always gives something up, so put the most important information first, since phone users decide quickly. Eric: phone and desktop must lead to the same result, so define the **3–5 core steps** a mobile/app user needs; the full platform holds the rest.
- **Test across devices and OSes.** Tony noted Mac and Windows render differently, and mobile differs again. Eric asked again about an app ("the next evolution").
- **Featured packages:** should they scroll as a carousel or stay a static grid? Open question.
- **Paystack:** is it connected, and is it where Ecobank virtual cards would be issued? This is needed for the bank pitch.
- **Domain:** find out what it costs to get TFE's own domain running again; it is currently hosted on Kevin's domain.

### 3. The proposed layout (Najenga / Naoa structure)
Kevin presented his standard landing structure: the page is built around **what you will do on the platform**. Sections cover who it's for, two highlight cards, stats, and interactive feature sections that preview the dashboard, including a mobile preview. There is also a dedicated walkthrough page per audience (fans, partners…) going step 1 → 2 → 3 → 4. The idea is to "give the tutorial before the user signs in", so users already know what to do when they reach a CTA, while still allowing a direct jump to the last step.

**Steven is sold** if Kevin thinks it improves the UX. His condition: **first settle who the partner categories are** (airlines, hotels, financing, schools/academies…) and build them into the framework so each stays visible. He likes the larger partner pages as **real estate to sell** to partners (cf. Tony's Ecobank presentation).

### 4. Partner and business discussion
- **Hotels as a partner category.** The GM of Convent Hotel (Convent Road) called Steven asking how to take part for AFCON. Steven wants to recruit hotels the way TFE recruits banks (JW Marriott, Four Points by Sheraton, Nyota Springs, Sheraton). The open questions are formal partnership and **commission on discovery through TFE**.
- **KTB (Kenya Tourism Board).** Eric thinks KTB backing first lends credibility (Mövenpick etc. will take TFE seriously). Tony disagrees: hotels just want sales and agents bring the business, so approach them with numbers (bed-nights × dates) and negotiate below rack and STO rates. Talk to them early, close later. Steven's synthesis: sign up hotels early, then go to KTB with "100 hotels already on board" so they endorse what exists.
- **Partner-side product.** Should hotels get access, or a report like "N fans from TFE are interested in your packages"? Should they be prompted to build packages?
- **Schools & academies** became very important in the Ecobank meeting. Schools could get a TFE page with a link to their own site.
- **Turkey contact (amputee football).** They are keen but busy hosting 1,200 guests and flying to the Amputee World Cup in Mexico (3 weeks). TFE is still waiting on tournament details, past photos and answers to Steven's questions. Once received, it will be packaged for the Ecobank pitch, and **the platform must be ready by then**.

### 5. Decisions
- Kevin will **build a repurposed version on the proposed layout AND keep the current version**. Both get tested, and the feedback is merged.
- A **small, closed tester group** (the team plus a few trusted people new to TFE) will test each version against a testing protocol Kevin sends.
- Kevin leads the platform work. Steven and Tony lead the business side (academies, schools), then call the next Ecobank meeting.
